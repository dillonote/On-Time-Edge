### Sections

**[body_text — Opening]**

One question.

When your schedule breaks on Wednesday — because demand shifted, a machine went down, or materials arrived late — does your team recover from the schedule, or despite it?

If the answer is "despite it," the schedule isn't accountable. It's decorative.

**[body_text — The distinction]**

An accountable schedule is a control mechanism. It reflects real constraints. It updates when conditions change. Teams run against it — not around it. When it breaks, recovery is managed through the schedule, not bypassed with spreadsheets and phone calls.

A decorative schedule is a plan that was accurate once. It sits in the APS. Planners export it, adjust it manually, and hope the floor follows. When disruption hits, the official schedule is the last thing anyone looks at.

One of these approaches produced a 96% reduction in scheduling time. A 60% reduction in order lead time. A 50% cut in overtime and freight costs.

The other produced your current Tuesday.

**[benefits — What makes a schedule accountable]**

The execution-ready checklist:

- Finite capacity — the schedule respects real resource availability, not infinite assumptions
- Closed-loop feedback — actuals from the floor update the schedule in near-real-time
- Constraint-aware — the binding constraint is visible, managed, and protected
- Scenario-driven — what-if analysis available in seconds, not hours
- Decision rights defined — who can change what, and under what conditions
- Operating cadence — daily/shift review rhythm anchored to the schedule
